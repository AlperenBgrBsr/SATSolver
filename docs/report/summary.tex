\begin{center}
{\Large\bfseries Where a CDCL SAT Solver Spends Its Time}\\[4pt]
Alperen Buğra Başar \quad\textbullet\quad Bilkent University
\quad\textbullet\quad \texttt{bugra.basar@ug.bilkent.edu.tr}\\
\url{https://github.com/AlperenBgrBsr/SATSolver}
\end{center}

I wrote a CDCL SAT solver from scratch in C++17 and measured where its time
goes, down to single instructions. Every answer it gives is checked
independently: satisfying assignments against the formula, and
unsatisfiability through a DRAT proof verified by \texttt{drat-trim}.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{A four-number cost model.} A skip costs \numCoefSkip{} cycles,
        a search for a new watched literal \numCoefSearch{}, each literal it
        checks \numCoefScan{}, and each conflict \numCoefConflict{}. Fitted
        over \numModelRuns{} runs, these predict total cycles with a median
        error of \numModelMedianErr\%.
  \item \textbf{A result that did not survive control.} One run made the
        solver look 12 times faster than MiniSat. Over 50 shuffled copies,
        the comparison flipped with the signs of the literals, and removing
        MiniSat's restarts and clause minimization made the two solvers
        statistically indistinguishable (conflict ratio
        \numGapNoflipNoRestartNoMin, 95\% interval spanning 1).
  \item \textbf{Memory, with the search held fixed.} Padding every clause grew
        the footprint \numPadFootprint{} times without changing a single
        decision. The cost per visit stays flat while the data fits in L2 and
        rises by \numPadSlowdown\% beyond it, only in code that reads the
        padded data.
  \item \textbf{Trust has a price, and it is small.} Proof logging adds
        \numProofOverheadMin--\numProofOverheadMax\% cycles, and checking a
        proof costs about as much as solving.
\end{itemize}
